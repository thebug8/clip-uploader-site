Privacy Policy

AutoYT · Last updated November 15, 2026

AutoYT is a personal tool that runs on its user's own computer. It has no hosted service, no accounts of its own and no analytics. This policy explains what it does with the Google account access you give it.

What the app accesses

When you link a YouTube channel, you grant the app permission through Google's sign-in screen to:

  • upload videos to that channel;
  • read the channel's name and picture, and the view, like and comment counts of its videos;
  • edit the title, description, tags, category and visibility of that channel's videos.

The app uses YouTube API Services to do this. By using it you also agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and your data is subject to the Google Privacy Policy.

How that access is used

Only to carry out the actions you take in the app: uploading a video you prepared, showing statistics for videos the app uploaded, and saving edits you make to those videos. The app does not read, change or delete anything else on your account.

What is stored, and where

Everything is stored on the computer the app runs on, and nowhere else:

  • the authorization token Google issues for each linked channel;
  • the channel's name, picture and ID;
  • a history of the uploads made through the app;
  • the video clips and drafts you are working on.

Sharing

None. The app sends data only to Google's and YouTube's own services, to perform the actions above. Nothing is sent to the developer or to any third party, and no data is sold or used for advertising.

The app's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and deleting data

  • Unlinking a channel in the app revokes its token and deletes it from the computer.
  • You can also remove the app's access at any time from your Google account's third-party access page.
  • Deleting the app's folder removes everything it has stored.

Questions

Questions about this policy can be raised as an issue on the GitHub repository that hosts this page.
